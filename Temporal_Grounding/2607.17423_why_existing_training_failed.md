# Why Existing Training Failed for Video Temporal Grounding

**Primary source:** Zhu et al., *TimeLens2: Generalist Video Temporal Grounding with Multimodal LLMs*, arXiv:2607.17423, 2026.  
**Focus of this note:** the *problem diagnosis* — why prior supervision and optimization pipelines are misaligned with the task. Solution details (TimeLens2-93K pipeline, temporal Wasserstein reward) are mentioned only where needed to clarify what was missing.

---

## 1. What the task actually is

### 1.1 Temporal grounding as citation, not captioning

Video multimodal large language models (MLLMs) are increasingly good at saying *what* happens in a video. They are much weaker at saying *when* the supporting evidence appears. TimeLens2 frames temporal grounding as the video analogue of citation: an answer is only trustworthy if it is tied to precise, potentially disjoint evidence intervals on the timeline (Zhu et al., 2026, §1).

Without temporal support:

- the user must still scrub the full video;
- even a fluent, factually correct description remains hard to verify;
- the model cannot be audited against visual evidence.

So the deliverable is not free-form video description. It is a mapping

\[
(v, q) \mapsto \mathcal{Y} = \{[s_k, e_k]\}_{k=1}^{K},
\]

where \(v\) is a video, \(q\) is a language query, and \(\mathcal{Y}\) is a **variable-cardinality set** of time intervals (possibly empty, single-span, or multi-span).

### 1.2 Why “generalist” is harder than classical moment retrieval

Classical temporal grounding / moment localization already existed before MLLMs (e.g., TALL on Charades-STA: Gao et al., 2017; DiDeMo: Hendricks et al., 2017; ActivityNet Captions: Krishna et al., 2017). Those settings were important, but comparatively narrow:

| Axis | Classical bias | Generalist requirement |
|------|----------------|------------------------|
| Video length | Often short / clipped | Seconds of evidence inside minutes–hours |
| Cardinality \(K\) | Usually one span | Repeated / disjoint evidence |
| Query form | Mostly declarative captions | Captions *and* questions |
| Viewpoint | Mostly third-person web/TV | Also egocentric (e.g., Ego4D-NLQ; Grauman et al., 2022) |
| Output interface | Dedicated localization head | Unified generative MLLM interface |

TimeLens2 therefore studies **generalist video temporal grounding**: one model, one output interface, variable-cardinality interval sets, across length, domain, query form, and viewpoint (Zhu et al., 2026, §1).

This definition matters. Many “failures of training” only appear once you leave the short-video single-span regime.

### 1.3 The mathematical object is a set of intervals

Two modeling consequences follow immediately:

1. **Cardinality is free.** The model may need 0, 1, or many intervals. Training that always assumes one box is incomplete.
2. **Partition is not identity.** The merged temporal support
   \[
   \mathrm{merge}(\mathcal{A}) = \bigcup_{[s,e]\in\mathcal{A}} [s,e]
   \]
   is often the semantically meaningful object. Splitting \([10,30]\) into \(\{[10,20],[20,30]\}\) need not change what evidence was recovered. Objectives that score *lists of boxes* as if partition were ground truth can punish equivalent answers.

TimeLens2’s diagnosis is that existing pipelines do not treat \(\mathcal{Y}\) as a first-class interval set **throughout** supervision *and* optimization (Zhu et al., 2026, Abstract & §1).

## 2. Two structural mismatches (the core claim)

TimeLens2 argues that progress was blocked by **two structural mismatches**, not by a missing backbone or a lack of scale alone (Zhu et al., 2026, §1):

1. **Supervision mismatch** — long-video labels are often produced as one brittle global decision; datasets under-represent multi-span, long-horizon, question-form, and egocentric search.
2. **Optimization mismatch** — next-token supervision teaches timestamp *syntax*; temporal IoU rewards only become informative after overlap; multi-span matching rewards reintroduce fragile correspondences.

The rest of this note expands both mismatches with evidence from TimeLens2 and from the papers it builds on.

---

## 3. Mismatch A — Supervision / data

### 3.1 Historical data shape: short, single, declarative

Early benchmarks primarily pair short or domain-specific videos with a **single** descriptive moment (Hendricks et al., 2017; Regneri et al., 2013; Gao et al., 2017; Krishna et al., 2017). Later datasets expand individual difficulty axes — subtitle-aware retrieval, highlights, long-form movie grounding, egocentric search (Lei et al., 2020; Lei et al., 2021; Soldan et al., 2022; Grauman et al., 2022) — but the *training mixture* used for many MLLMs still inherits a strong single-span, caption-style prior.

Consequence for learning:

- the model practices boundary regression more than **evidence search among distractors**;
- multi-event completeness is rarely supervised;
- transfer to question-form localization and hour-scale video remains weak even when short-video scores look strong.

### 3.2 Long-video annotation is an evidence-verification problem

TimeLens2’s sharpest data claim: for long videos, annotation is not merely expensive scaling — it is **epistemically hard** (Zhu et al., 2026, §1, §3.1).

Why one-pass global annotation fails:

| Failure mode | Mechanism |
|--------------|-----------|
| **Wrong occurrence** | The queried action appears more than once; similar distractors dominate the timeline; the annotator marks the wrong instance. |
| **Missed repeats** | Only the first (or most salient) hit is labeled; later supporting moments are omitted. |
| **Imprecise boundaries** | Evidence is sparse; transitions are subtle; start/end times drift by seconds. |

Relevant moments are sparse relative to duration. Full-video context is needed to *find* evidence, yet that same long context is exactly what makes a single global labeling pass unreliable. TimeLens2 states this tension directly: full-video context is required to teach search, while sparse evidence and growing distractors make precise annotation unreliable (Zhu et al., 2026, §3.1).

So “just annotate more long videos the usual way” does not automatically yield trustworthy multi-span supervision.

### 3.3 Scale without verification does not fix quality

MLLM recipes often convert timestamped annotations into instruction conversations (Ren et al., 2023; Huang et al., 2024a; Zeng et al., 2025; Qian et al., 2024), and further scale via dense captions, procedural video, or weak narration alignment (e.g., HowTo100M-style and instructional corpora; Miech et al., 2019; Zhou et al., 2017; Tang et al., 2019).

TimeLens2’s point is not that scaling is useless. It is that **scale does not by itself resolve supervision quality**: in long videos, coarse alignment readily becomes a wrong occurrence, a missed repeat, or an imprecise boundary (Zhu et al., 2026, §2).

This is reinforced by the prior TimeLens study, which systematically examined data quality and training recipes for MLLM temporal grounding and found serious quality issues in existing VTG benchmarks and training data — severe enough that re-annotation changes model rankings (Zhang et al., 2026; *TimeLens*). If evaluation labels are noisy, reported “SOTA” can partly measure fit to noise.

### 3.4 Empirical signature inside TimeLens2: quality ≫ raw quantity

Although this note prioritizes problem understanding, one ablation is diagnostic of the *data problem*, not of the later method:

- Raw dual-agent labels at very large scale (~735K–999K QA turns) only reach ~42.0 average mIoU under fixed SFT.
- Temporal consensus + semantic verification **throws away most labels** (down to 93.2K) and *improves* average mIoU.
- Boundary refinement on the *same* 93.2K instances yields the largest stage gain (+1.7), showing that once event identity is correct, residual error is boundary noise (Zhu et al., 2026, Table 3).

Interpretation: the bottleneck was not “not enough strings of timestamps,” but **which interval sets are trustworthy**. A pipeline that cannot factor annotation error into (i) temporal reproducibility, (ii) query–evidence relevance, and (iii) boundary precision will keep feeding the optimizer corrupted targets.

### 3.5 Coverage gaps remain even when labels are locally correct

Even clean single-domain labels leave transfer holes:

- **Question-form** queries (e.g., MomentSeeker; Yuan et al., 2026) differ from declarative captions used in most grounding corpora.
- **Egocentric** search (Ego4D-NLQ; Grauman et al., 2022) differs from third-person web video.
- **Long-horizon multi-span** retrieval (e.g., VUE-TR / VUE-TR-V2 style settings; Vidi Team et al., 2025a, 2025b) differs from short Charades-like clips.

A model can look strong on legacy short-video splits while still failing the generalist regime TimeLens2 cares about. That is a *curriculum and coverage* failure, not only a loss-function failure.

## 4. Mismatch B — Optimization / learning signal

Assume, for a moment, that labels are perfect. Training can still fail because the objective does not score interval sets the way the task does.

### 4.1 Stage I failure: supervised fine-tuning optimizes tokens, not intervals

Generative MLLM grounding systems (TimeChat, VTimeLLM, LITA, Grounded-VideoLLM, TimeSuite, etc.) cast localization as text generation: the model emits timestamp tokens in natural language or structured formats (Ren et al., 2023; Huang et al., 2024a; Huang et al., 2024b; Wang et al., 2025a; Zeng et al., 2025).

Training loss is standard next-token likelihood.

**What SFT does well**

- teaches the model that grounding answers should contain times;
- aligns the language interface (JSON, “from … to …”, etc.);
- can imprint coarse video–query associations when data are clean.

**What SFT does not do**

- it does not directly optimize temporal overlap or set coverage;
- token-level errors do not mirror geometric errors on the timeline  
  (being 2 seconds early vs 20 minutes off is not represented as “distance”);
- it provides no explicit pressure to *search* a long context rather than pattern-match a plausible time string.

TimeLens2 states this bluntly: supervised instruction tuning teaches the **syntax** of timestamps through token likelihood, not the **quality** of localized evidence (Zhu et al., 2026, §2).

Related empirical pattern in the RL-for-VTG literature: large LVLMs trained mainly with SFT can still underperform much smaller specialized grounding models on temporal localization, and SFT-only “cold start” is often insufficient for robust generalization (Wang et al., 2025c, *Time-R1* / TimeZero line of work; Chen et al., 2025, *TVG-R1*). TimeZero-style analyses further note that query-relevant content may occupy only a small fraction of a long video, so direct timestamp regression without an explicit search/reasoning pressure is a poor fit (Wang et al., 2025c).

**Conceptual summary:** SFT learns *how to write an answer that looks like grounding*; it does not reliably learn *how to place an interval set*.

### 4.2 Stage II response: RL with temporal IoU — better aligned, still incomplete

To close the metric gap, recent work applies reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR), often GRPO (Shao et al., 2024), using temporal Intersection-over-Union (tIoU) plus format rewards (Wang et al., 2025c; Chen et al., 2025; Li et al., 2025b; Guo-style and follow-on VTG-RL recipes; Zhang et al., 2026; and multi-segment extensions such as Luo et al., 2026).

For a single predicted interval \([t_s, t_e]\) and ground truth \([t_s', t_e']\):

\[
\mathrm{tIoU}
=
\frac{\bigl|[t_s,t_e]\cap[t_s',t_e']\bigr|}{\bigl|[t_s,t_e]\cup[t_s',t_e']\bigr|}.
\]

For sets, TimeLens2 (and several contemporaries) lift this to merged supports:

\[
R_{\mathrm{tIoU}}(\hat{\mathcal{Y}},\mathcal{Y})
=
\frac{\bigl|\mathrm{merge}(\hat{\mathcal{Y}})\cap\mathrm{merge}(\mathcal{Y})\bigr|}{\bigl|\mathrm{merge}(\hat{\mathcal{Y}})\cup\mathrm{merge}(\mathcal{Y})\bigr|}.
\]

This is a real improvement over pure SFT: the training signal finally mentions overlap, which is what benchmarks report (R1@μ, mIoU).

### 4.3 The tIoU plateau: zero is not graded

The decisive limitation: **overlap supplies geometry only after prediction and target intersect** (Zhu et al., 2026, §2, §3.2).

Consider two non-overlapping predictions:

```text
Timeline:   |------------[==== GT ====]------------------|

Near miss:  |--------[==]--------------------------------|
Far miss:   |-------------------------------[==]---------|
```

Both receive \(\mathrm{tIoU} = 0\). As a *reward*, tIoU cannot express:

- “A is close; shift right,” versus
- “B is in the wrong region; jump.”

Near miss and far miss are **tied**. During early or hard search — exactly when the policy most needs directional feedback — the reward is flat.

TimeLens2 also notes a multi-moment pathology: tIoU can prefer a prediction that covers one target moment well over a prediction that partially covers *both* moments, depending on merge/union arithmetic (Zhu et al., 2026, Fig. 3b). So even among overlapping answers, scalar overlap is an incomplete ranking of set-quality.

### 4.4 Why this is fatal under GRPO-style group learning

GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) samples a group of completions for one prompt and learns from **relative** advantages inside the group (mean-centered rewards; often without a learned critic).

If every sample in the group is non-overlapping:

- all tIoU rewards are 0;
- mean-centered advantages collapse;
- the update receives **no temporal preference**.

TimeLens2 quantifies this failure mode on real RL rollouts (Zhu et al., 2026, Table 9):

- under \(R_{\mathrm{tIoU}}\) alone, **13.8%** of valid groups are constant-reward (no within-group ranking);
- among all-zero-tIoU groups, adding a distance-aware temporal term later **rescues 75.8%** into non-constant groups.

You do not need to accept their final reward design to accept the diagnosis: **with tIoU-only rewards, a large mass of on-policy failures is invisible to GRPO.**

This is not a minor implementation bug. It is a structural mismatch between:

- a metric that is excellent for *evaluation once candidates already overlap*, and
- a learning signal that must rank *imperfect localizations during search*.

### 4.5 Multi-span “dense” rewards via matching: another broken representation

For multi-segment targets, one response to tIoU sparsity is pairwise matching + a generalized IoU (e.g., MUSEG’s NGIoU under one-to-one matching; Luo et al., 2026).

The intent is right: give graded credit when segments do not overlap, and handle multiple segments. TimeLens2 argues the *representation* is still wrong (Zhu et al., 2026, §2, §3.2, Fig. 3c–d):

1. **Fragmentation artifact**  
   If the model splits one event into two predicted spans, a forced one-to-one matcher may assign a fragment to the wrong target event.

2. **Merge / unequal cardinality**  
   If the model merges two events, or predicts a different number of spans than GT, leftover or double-booked assignments distort the score.

3. **Partition dependence**  
   Two predictions with **identical merged support** can receive very different matched-NGIoU scores solely because one is written as one interval and the other as two. TimeLens2’s illustrative case: identical support scored **1.0 vs 0.32** under different partitions (Zhu et al., 2026, Fig. 3d).

So the reward starts measuring **how the set was serialized**, not only **what temporal mass was recovered**. That conflicts with the set-valued nature of the task (§1.3).

Empirically, under TimeLens2’s controlled RL ablations, matched NGIoU underperforms their match-free alternative especially on multi-interval long-video benchmarks (Zhu et al., 2026, Table 7) — consistent with the claim that matching fragility is not hypothetical.

### 4.6 Secondary optimization issues (real, but subordinate)

These are not TimeLens2’s main theorem, but they compound Mismatch B:

- **Interface entanglement.** A single prompt/timestamp format lets the model learn surface form instead of localization. TimeLens2 shows request/rendering diversity improves transfer (Table 5), which implies prior single-template SFT was partly teaching the interface.
- **Context truncation / short-context SFT.** If training never forces full-video search, the model never practices distractor rejection. Their packed-length ablation rises from 16K → 100K tokens with uneven gains concentrated on long/hard sets (Table 4) — evidence that many recipes under-trained the search horizon.
- **Uniform RL sampling.** If GRPO spends updates on already-solved easy prompts, hard non-overlap cases (where tIoU is silent) remain untrained. Hard-sample mining is a response to this (Zhu et al., 2026, §3.2); the underlying problem is wasted signal on the wrong difficulty band.

## 5. How the mismatches interact (failure cascade)

The two mismatches are not independent. They form a cascade:

```text
Task needs: variable-cardinality interval sets on long, distractor-heavy video
                    │
                    ▼
┌───────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ DATA: one-pass / weak / single-span labels      │
│  → wrong occurrence, missed repeats, fuzzy ends │
└───────────────────────────────────────────────┘
                    │
                    ▼
┌───────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ SFT: next-token imitation of timestamp text     │
│  → fluent formats, weak geometric placement     │
└───────────────────────────────────────────────┘
                    │
                    ▼
┌───────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ RL + tIoU: informative only after overlap       │
│  → near miss ≡ far miss ≡ 0                     │
│  → GRPO groups go silent                        │
└───────────────────────────────────────────────┘
                    │
                    ▼
┌───────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ Multi-span matching rewards (optional patch)  │
│  → fragmentation / merge / partition artifacts│
└───────────────────────────────────────────────┘
                    │
                    ▼
Brittle transfer: short-video scores can look fine while
long / multi-span / question / ego grounding stay weak
```

**Important reading instruction:** fixing only the last box (reward) while keeping bad labels still teaches the wrong targets. Fixing only labels while keeping token loss + flat tIoU still under-teaches search. TimeLens2’s claim is that **both ends** must treat evidence as an interval set (Zhu et al., 2026, §5).

## 6. What prior lines of work already revealed (and what they left open)

This section situates TimeLens2’s diagnosis historically so the problem is not mistaken for a brand-new slogan.

| Line of work | What it improved | What remained open (problem residue) |
|--------------|------------------|--------------------------------------|
| Classical proposal/regression grounding (Gao et al., 2017; etc.) | Explicit localization heads, tIoU-centric eval | Narrow regimes; not a general MLLM capability |
| Time-aware MLLM SFT (TimeChat, VTimeLLM, LITA, TimeSuite, …) | Timestamp language interface; instruction tuning | Token loss ≠ interval quality; limited generalist transfer |
| Data-quality push (TimeLens; Zhang et al., 2026) | Shows benchmark/train label noise; cleaner recipes | Overlap-centric RL still inherits tIoU plateaus |
| RLVR / GRPO for VTG (Time-R1/TimeZero, TVG-R1, VideoChat-R1, …) | Metric-aligned overlap rewards beat SFT alone | Non-overlap ties; group-relative silence; multi-span under-specified |
| Multi-segment RL (MUSEG; Luo et al., 2026) | Dense multi-span attempts via matching + NGIoU | Matching fragile under split/merge/unequal \(K\) |

TimeLens2’s distinctive *problem statement* is that these residues share one root: **the community optimized proxies (tokens, single boxes, pairwise matches) instead of set-valued temporal evidence end-to-end.**

## 7. Concrete walk-through (why the old signals mis-rank)

**Ground truth (multi-span):** handcuff events at \([100,105]\) and \([400,404]\).

| Prediction | Human quality | tIoU (merged) | Matched multi-span risk | What a good set-objective should say |
|------------|---------------|---------------|-------------------------|--------------------------------------|
| \([100,105],[400,404]\) | Correct | High | Low if perfectly aligned | Best |
| \([98,103]\) only | Right neighborhood, incomplete | Medium / low | May look “OK” on first event only | Penalize missing second mass; keep first |
| \([90,95]\) only | Near miss on first | **0** | Pairwise distance could help *if* matched | Prefer over far miss |
| \([250,255]\) only | Far miss | **0** | Same 0 as near miss under pure tIoU | Worse than near miss |
| \([100,404]\) one blob | Covers both, huge false interior | Medium | One-to-one oddities | Prefer split true events over giant union |
| \([100,105],[105,404]\) vs one \([100,404]\) | Similar support, different cuts | Similar | **Scores can diverge** under matching | Should be closer than serialization suggests |

Under pure tIoU RL, rows “near miss” and “far miss” are tied at zero — no curriculum toward the target.  
Under matched list rewards, rows with equivalent support but different fragmentation can be ranked inconsistently.  
Under SFT alone, all of these might be “almost the same token sequence length” with no geometric ordering at all.

This table is the practical content of TimeLens2 Figure 3, restated for study.

## 8. Diagnostic checklist (how to recognize the failure in a new paper)

When reading another temporal-grounding MLLM paper, the TimeLens2 diagnosis suggests asking:

1. **Label object:** Are targets single intervals only? Who annotated long multi-span evidence, and how was agreement checked?
2. **Search vs crop:** Does training ever require selecting sparse evidence from full long videos, or only boundary refine on trimmed clips?
3. **SFT metric gap:** Is the only training loss next-token CE on timestamp text?
4. **RL zero plateau:** If RL is used, what fraction of rollouts have zero overlap? Does the reward distinguish them?
5. **Set representation:** For multi-span outputs, does the reward depend on a brittle match or on merged support?
6. **Eval breadth:** Do gains live only on short single-span legacy sets, or also on long / multi-span / question / ego splits?

If (3)–(5) are weak, strong leaderboard numbers on Charades-like sets may overstate generalist grounding ability.

## 9. Condensed problem statement

**Existing training failed because it never consistently optimized the true task object.**

- **Data side:** long-video supervision was treated as one-shot labeling and short-video single-span corpora were over-trusted, producing wrong occurrences, missed repeats, fuzzy boundaries, and coverage holes across query forms and viewpoints (Zhu et al., 2026; Zhang et al., 2026; classical dataset lineage in Gao et al., 2017; Hendricks et al., 2017; Krishna et al., 2017).
- **Learning side:** SFT optimized timestamp *syntax* (Ren et al., 2023; Huang et al., 2024a; Zeng et al., 2025), while tIoU-based RL optimized *overlap only after intersection* (Wang et al., 2025c; Chen et al., 2025; Shao et al., 2024), and matched multi-span rewards reintroduced fragile correspondences (Luo et al., 2026).

Until both supervision and optimization treat evidence as a **variable-cardinality interval set** — verifiable as labels, geometric as a learning signal — models will continue to speak fluently about video while remaining unreliable at pointing to when the evidence occurs.

## Appendix — Scope note

This write-up intentionally **under-describes** TimeLens2’s remedies (staged consensus labeling; temporal Wasserstein on uniform merged mass; SFT→GRPO recipe). Those matter for engineering reproduction, but they are responses to the failure modes above. If the problem statement in §§1–5 is clear, the motivation for any later geometric set-reward and verification-based dataset construction should feel inevitable rather than ornamental.
