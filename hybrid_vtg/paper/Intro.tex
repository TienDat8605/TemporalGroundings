\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Video Temporal Grounding (VTG) aims to identify start and end timestamps in untrimmed video corresponding to a natural language query~\cite{gao2017tall,anne2017localizing}. While traditional VTG benchmarks focus on the single-instance setting where each query matches exactly one moment, real-world videos frequently contain recurring actions. In domains such as surveillance, sports analytics, and video search, an action described by a query (e.g., \emph{"the athlete drinks water"} or \emph{"person opens the refrigerator"}) often repeats multiple times across non-contiguous intervals.

To evaluate this capability, recent work introduced \textbf{One-to-Many Video Temporal Grounding (OMTG)}~\cite{omtg2026}. Unlike traditional single-span retrieval, OMTG requires both: (1) \emph{cardinality perception} (predicting the total number of event occurrences $K \ge 1$), and (2) \emph{boundary localization} (predicting temporal timestamps for each event). Current Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs), including Gemini-1.5-Pro, Video-LLaVA~\cite{lin2023videollava}, and TimeLens-8B~\cite{timelens2024}, struggle on OMTG, obtaining $0.00\%$ Count Accuracy (C-Acc) and $0.00\%$ Effective Temporal F1 (EtF1) under standard prompts in official evaluations~\cite{omtg2026}.

In this paper, we analyze the performance drop of MLLMs on OMTG and find two primary causes:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Prompt Format Misalignment}: Default benchmark prompts embed output instructions within unstructured natural language dialogue. This causes instruction-tuned models to output conversational text or a single timestamp instead of structured multi-span lists.
    \item \textbf{Background Frames in Long Videos}: In long untrimmed videos ($100\text{s}-500\text{s}$), uniform sampling forces the Vision Transformer to process thousands of irrelevant background frames, which can trigger false positive detections and degrade event counting accuracy.
\end{enumerate}

To address both issues without parameter fine-tuning, we propose \textbf{Adaptive Scout-Guided Dense Evidence (Adaptive SGDE)}, a training-free framework for multi-event temporal grounding. As shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:pipeline_intro}, Adaptive SGDE uses a four-stage coarse-to-fine pipeline:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Stage 1: Coarse Multimodal Scouting}: A frozen 1.04B embedding scout scans the untrimmed video at 1.0~FPS, producing a cross-modal similarity timeline normalized by video-level $Z$-scores to handle domain baseline shifts.
    \item \textbf{Stage 2: Hysteresis Energy Mining}: Dual-threshold hysteresis search ($\tau_{\text{high}}=0.80, \tau_{\text{low}}=0.25$) combined with normalized excess energy scoring $\bar{J}(a,b)$ extracts candidate action intervals.
    \item \textbf{Stage 3: Duration-Adaptive Windowing}: Sub-linear context margins ($\Delta(T) \propto \ln T$) and clustering gaps ($G(T) \propto \sqrt{T}$) cluster candidates across diverse video lengths ($12\text{s}-500\text{s}$), unified by a $70\%$ soft continuity merge rule to maintain cross-event context.
    \item \textbf{Stage 4: Focused Dense Grounding}: The primary grounder (TimeLens-8B~\cite{timelens2024}) processes only the candidate window under a structured JSON prompt, focusing frame allocation on relevant action regions.
\end{itemize}

Evaluations on the complete OMTG benchmark ($N=320$) show that Adaptive SGDE improves training-free grounding, increasing Count Accuracy from $0.00\% \rightarrow \mathbf{33.12\%}$ ($+13.43\%$ over prompt-only baselines) and Effective Temporal F1 to $\mathbf{19.86\%}$ (+19.86 points over the official paper baseline). Furthermore, by removing $50\%-85\%$ of irrelevant background intervals, Adaptive SGDE reduces 8B Vision Transformer frame evaluations by $\mathbf{60.3\%}$, achieving a net $\mathbf{-54.0\%}$ inference compute reduction.
