\begin{abstract}
Conventional Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) are trained predominantly for single-event retrieval. When evaluated on \textbf{One-to-Many Video Temporal Grounding (OMTG)}---where a natural language query corresponds to multiple disjoint temporal intervals across long videos---standard MLLMs experience catastrophic cardinality collapse ($0.00\%$ Count Accuracy in official evaluations). In this paper, we propose \textbf{Adaptive Scout-Guided Dense Evidence (Adaptive SGDE)}, a training-free, zero-shot framework that resolves the OMTG cardinality bottleneck without parameter updates. We demonstrate that MLLM cardinality failure stems from two distinct factors: prompt format misalignment and background distractor pollution in unpruned long videos. By combining schema-delimited prompt restructuring with an ultra-lightweight 1.04B temporal scout at 1.0~FPS, sub-linear duration-adaptive geometry ($\Delta(T) \propto \ln T$, $G(T) \propto \sqrt{T}$), and $70\%$ soft continuity merging, our pipeline dynamically prunes $50\%-85\%$ of background dead-zones. On the full OMTG benchmark ($N=320$), Adaptive SGDE boosts Count Accuracy from $0.00\%$ to $33.12\%$ and Effective Temporal F1 from $0.00\%$ to $19.86\%$, while reducing primary 8B Vision Transformer frame evaluations by $60.3\%$ (yielding a net $-54.0\%$ end-to-end inference compute reduction).

\keywords{Video Temporal Grounding \and Multimodal LLMs \and One-to-Many Grounding \and Duration-Adaptive Windowing \and Training-Free Inference.}
\end{abstract}
