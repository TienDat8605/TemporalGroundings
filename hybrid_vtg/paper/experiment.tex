\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:exp_setup}

\paragraph{Benchmark Dataset.} We evaluate on the complete \textbf{OMTG Bench}~\cite{omtg2026}, the standard benchmark for One-to-Many Video Temporal Grounding. The benchmark consists of 320 manually verified query-video pairs spanning 287 untrimmed videos from Charades, ActivityNet, QVHighlights, VTimeLLM, and Moment10M. Durations range from $12.0\text{s}$ to $506.0\text{s}$ (mean: $161.4\text{s}$), with ground-truth instance counts ranging from 2 to 20 per query (mean: 3.67 instances).

\paragraph{Evaluation Metrics.} Following the OMTG protocol~\cite{omtg2026}, we report:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Count Accuracy (C-Acc, \%) $\uparrow$}: Percentage of samples where the predicted segment count exactly matches ground-truth cardinality ($M=K$).
    \item \textbf{Temporal F1 (tF1@$\xi$, \%) $\uparrow$}: Harmonic mean of precision and recall under Hungarian bipartite matching at IoU thresholds $\xi \in \{0.3, 0.5, 0.7\}$, penalizing unmatched predictions as false alarms.
    \item \textbf{Temporal IoU (tIoU, \%) $\uparrow$}: Mean temporal intersection-over-union across matched ground-truth and predicted intervals.
    \item \textbf{Effective Temporal F1 (EtF1, \%) $\uparrow$}: The primary OMTG metric, which conditions temporal F1 on exact cardinality match ($\text{EtF1} = \text{tF1} \times \mathbf{1}(M=K)$).
    \item \textbf{Cardinality Error $\downarrow$}: Mean absolute error between predicted and ground-truth event counts ($|M - K|$).
    \item \textbf{Processed Frames and Compute Saved (\%) $\uparrow$}: Aggregate video frames processed by the 8B Vision Transformer across all 320 evaluations ($N_{\text{frames}} = \sum_i F_i$), and relative frame reduction $\text{Saved} = (1 - N_{\text{frames}}^{\text{method}} / N_{\text{frames}}^{\text{native}}) \times 100\%$.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Implementation Details.} Our primary grounder is frozen TimeLens-8B~\cite{timelens2024} (Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct backbone). The temporal scout is frozen Llama-Nemotron-Embed-VL-1B (1.04B parameters) sampled at 1.0~FPS. Hyperparameters are fixed across all experiments: $\tau_{\text{high}}=0.80$, $\tau_{\text{low}}=0.25$, $\tau_{\text{base}}=0.30$, $\lambda_{\text{len}}=0.01$, $\alpha_{\text{margin}}=3.5\text{s}$, $\beta_{\text{gap}}=3.5\text{s}$, and $\rho=0.70$.

Table~\ref{tab:main_results} and Fig.~\ref{fig:video_length_pruning} present grounding accuracy and compute efficiency on OMTG Bench. Four primary insights emerge:

\paragraph{1. Prompt Syntax vs. Latent Capacity.} Under the unstructured prompt used in the official OMTG study~\cite{omtg2026}, TimeLens-8B completely collapses on multi-span events ($0.00\%$ C-Acc and $0.00\%$ EtF1). Enforcing schema-delimited JSON syntax unlocks latent multi-span localization, recovering $19.69\%$ C-Acc and $14.23\%$ EtF1. This reveals that reported baseline failures stem substantially from syntactic misalignment rather than lack of grounding capability.

\paragraph{2. Video Length Scaling and Dead-Zone Pruning.} As shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:video_length_pruning}, benchmark videos span wide temporal ranges ($12.0\text{s}-506.0\text{s}$). When fed raw, unpruned long videos, models hallucinate across uninformative background segments. Adaptive SGDE dynamically preserves short videos ($0\%$ pruned for $T \le 45\text{s}$) while pruning an average of \textbf{58.1\% of background frames} on longer videos (rising to $69.5\%$ on videos $>180\text{s}$ and up to $85.2\%$). Eliminating these dead-zones enables Adaptive SGDE-128 to boost Count Accuracy to \textbf{33.12\%} ($+13.43\%$ over the prompt baseline) and reduce Cardinality Error from $2.07 \rightarrow \textbf{1.75}$.

\paragraph{3. Approaching the Theoretical Ceiling.} To quantify the performance upper bound of the frozen TimeLens-8B grounder under a 128-frame budget, we evaluate a \emph{Theoretical Limit} baseline using an Oracle ground-truth corridor ($100\%$ GT span retention, $0\%$ background false alarms). As reported in Table~\ref{tab:main_results}, this oracle ceiling achieves $33.44\%$ C-Acc and $20.99\%$ EtF1. Completely training-free and operating without ground-truth access, our proposed Adaptive SGDE-128 closely approaches this oracle ceiling, achieving \textbf{33.12\% vs. 33.44\% C-Acc} (within $0.32\%$) and \textbf{19.14\% vs. 20.99\% EtF1}, demonstrating that training-free semantic scouting successfully isolates informative event regions without requiring supervision.

\paragraph{4. Precision-Recall Dynamics and Compute Savings.} The prompt baseline displays high precision ($60.70\%$) but low recall ($40.84\%$) due to conservative single-instance under-prediction ($0\%$ multi-event C-Acc). Adaptive SGDE actively exposes multiple candidate intervals, elevating Recall to $44.11\%$ and setting a new state of the art at \textbf{19.86\% EtF1} with 256 frames. Simultaneously, concentrating 8B ViT decoding solely on candidate corridors slashes heavy frame evaluations by \textbf{60.3\%} (consuming 128 fixed frames vs. $322.8\text{f}$ average on raw video), offloading initial temporal search to the lightweight 1.04B scout.

\paragraph{1. Semantic Guidance vs. Random Windowing.} As shown in Table~\ref{tab:ablation_windowing}, evaluating a random temporal window matching the average candidate duration ratio ($40\%$) collapses EtF1 to \textbf{3.61\%} and tIoU to \textbf{24.18\%}. This demonstrates that temporal windowing without cross-modal semantic guidance severely degrades grounding fidelity.

\paragraph{2. Single-Window vs. Multi-Window Topology.} Transitioning from raw video to a single continuous scout corridor (strategy b) improves Count Accuracy from $16.88\% \rightarrow \textbf{28.44\%}$ and EtF1 from $12.29\% \rightarrow \textbf{16.86\%}$. However, forcing distant multi-event clusters into one window retains unpruned intermediate background, capping tIoU at $36.81\%$. While naively splitting intervals without soft merging (strategy c) fragments adjacent moments ($16.81\%$ EtF1), our proposed **Adaptive SGDE** with $70\%$ soft continuity merging (strategy d) dynamically unifies proximate clusters while isolating distant intervals, boosting tIoU to \textbf{44.76\%}, Count Accuracy to \textbf{33.12\%}, and EtF1 to \textbf{19.14\%}.

\paragraph{3. Timeline Scoring Function Robustness.} As depicted in Fig.~\ref{fig:scoring_ablation}, naive cosine similarity with a fixed cutoff suffers from baseline similarity shifts across video domains, causing $25.1\%$ missed action spans. Video-adaptive $Z$-score normalization combined with dual-threshold hysteresis ($\tau_{\text{high}}=0.80, \tau_{\text{low}}=0.25$) and excess energy integral $J(a,b)$ raises ground-truth coverage from $70.9\% \rightarrow \textbf{80.3\%}$ ($+9.4\%$), improves proposal IoU from $34.2\% \rightarrow \textbf{48.6\%}$, and cuts missed action spans by $31.1\%$ relative.

\paragraph{4. Soft Merge Span Ratio Optimization ($\rho$).} Fig.~\ref{fig:soft_merge_ablation} plots the continuity-versus-pruning trade-off across merge ratio thresholds $\rho \in [30\%, 100\%]$. Low thresholds ($\rho \le 40\%$) preserve all videos in 1-window prompts but prune fewer background frames ($33.3\%$). High thresholds ($\rho \ge 90\%$) or gap-only merging fragment $7.5\%-14.7\%$ of videos across multiple disjoint prompts, disrupting relational multi-event context. Setting $\rho = 70\%$ achieves the optimal trade-off: keeping \textbf{95.0\% of videos in a single continuous prompt} while capturing \textbf{79.71\% of GT action spans} and pruning \textbf{37.17\% of background frames} overall (up to $85.2\%$ on videos $>180\text{s}$).
