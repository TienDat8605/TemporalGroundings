\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:exp_setup}

\paragraph{Benchmark Dataset.} We evaluate on the complete \textbf{OMTG Bench}~\cite{omtg2026}, the established benchmark for One-to-Many Video Temporal Grounding. The benchmark consists of 320 manually verified query-video pairs spanning 287 untrimmed videos from Charades, ActivityNet, QVHighlights, VTimeLLM, and Moment10M. Durations range from $12.0\text{s}$ to $506.0\text{s}$ (mean: $161.4\text{s}$), with ground-truth instance counts ranging from 2 to 20 per query (mean: 3.67 instances).

\paragraph{Evaluation Metrics.} Following the official OMTG evaluation protocol~\cite{omtg2026}, we report:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Count Accuracy (C-Acc, \%) $\uparrow$}: Percentage of samples where the predicted segment count exactly matches ground-truth cardinality ($M=K$).
    \item \textbf{Temporal F1 (tF1@$\xi$, \%) $\uparrow$}: Harmonic mean of precision and recall under Hungarian bipartite matching at IoU thresholds $\xi \in \{0.3, 0.5, 0.7\}$, with unmatched predictions penalized as false alarms.
    \item \textbf{Temporal IoU (tIoU, \%) $\uparrow$}: Mean temporal intersection-over-union across matched ground-truth and predicted intervals.
    \item \textbf{Effective Temporal F1 (EtF1, \%) $\uparrow$}: The primary benchmark metric, which conditions temporal F1 on exact cardinality match ($\text{EtF1} = \text{tF1} \times \mathbf{1}(M=K)$).
    \item \textbf{Cardinality Error $\downarrow$}: Mean absolute error between predicted and ground-truth event counts ($|M - K|$).
    \item \textbf{Processed Frames and Compute Saved (\%) $\uparrow$}: Aggregate video frames processed by the 8B Vision Transformer across all 320 evaluations ($N_{\text{frames}} = \sum_i F_i$), and relative frame reduction $\text{Saved} = (1 - N_{\text{frames}}^{\text{method}} / N_{\text{frames}}^{\text{native}}) \times 100\%$.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Implementation Details.} Our primary grounder is frozen TimeLens-8B~\cite{timelens2024} (Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct backbone). The temporal scout is frozen Llama-Nemotron-Embed-VL-1B (1.04B parameters) sampled at a coarse 1.0~FPS. Hyperparameters are fixed across all benchmark videos without dataset-specific tuning: detection thresholds $\tau_{\text{high}}=0.80, \tau_{\text{low}}=0.25$ ($\S$\ref{sec:stage2}), energy baseline offset $\tau_{\text{base}}=0.30$ (Eq.~\ref{eq:excess_energy}), adaptive window coefficients $\alpha_{\text{margin}}=3.5\text{s}, \beta_{\text{gap}}=3.5\text{s}$ (Eq.~\ref{eq:adaptive_geometry}), and merge threshold $\rho=0.70$ (Eq.~\ref{eq:soft_merge}).

Table~\ref{tab:main_results} and Fig.~\ref{fig:video_length_pruning} present grounding accuracy and compute efficiency on OMTG Bench. Four primary insights emerge:

\paragraph{1. Prompt Syntax vs. Grounding Capacity.} Under the unstructured prompt used in the official OMTG study~\cite{omtg2026}, TimeLens-8B outputs conversational fragments or single moments, resulting in 0.00\% Count Accuracy and 0.00\% Effective Temporal F1. Enforcing schema-delimited JSON syntax restores multi-span output, reaching 19.69\% Count Accuracy and 14.23\% Effective Temporal F1. This indicates that baseline failure in prior benchmarks was partly caused by prompt formatting rather than model capacity.

\paragraph{2. Video Length Scaling and Background Removal.} As shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:video_length_pruning}, benchmark videos range from 12.0s to 506.0s. On long unpruned videos, models often make false positive detections across uninformative background segments. Adaptive SGDE preserves short videos while removing an average of \textbf{58.1\% of background frames} on longer videos. Removing these background segments enables Adaptive SGDE-128 to increase Count Accuracy to \textbf{33.12\%} and reduce Cardinality Error from 2.07 to \textbf{1.75}.

\paragraph{3. Comparison with Oracle Ground-Truth Window Reference.} To provide a performance reference for the frozen TimeLens-8B grounder under a 128-frame budget, we evaluate an oracle reference where frames are sampled within the ground-truth action envelope. As reported in Table~\ref{tab:main_results}, this oracle reference achieves 33.44\% Count Accuracy and 20.99\% Effective Temporal F1. Without access to ground-truth annotations, our training-free Adaptive SGDE-128 achieves \textbf{33.12\% vs. 33.44\% Count Accuracy} and \textbf{19.14\% vs. 20.99\% Effective Temporal F1}, demonstrating that the lightweight scout effectively identifies informative video regions.

\paragraph{4. Precision-Recall Dynamics and Compute Savings.} The prompt baseline exhibits higher precision (60.70\%) but low recall (40.84\%) because it under-predicts multiple instances. Adaptive SGDE exposes candidate action intervals across the focused window, increasing Recall to 44.11\% and reaching \textbf{19.86\% Effective Temporal F1} with 256 frames. In addition, focusing 8B model decoding on candidate windows reduces frame evaluations by \textbf{60.3\%}, offloading initial temporal search to the lightweight 1.04B scout.

\paragraph{1. Semantic Guidance vs. Random Windowing.} As shown in Table~\ref{tab:ablation_windowing}, evaluating a random temporal window matching the average candidate duration ratio of 40\% drops Effective Temporal F1 to \textbf{3.61\%} and tIoU to \textbf{24.18\%}. This shows that temporal windowing without semantic guidance degrades grounding performance.

\paragraph{2. Single-Window vs. Multi-Window Topology.} Moving from raw video to a single continuous window (strategy b) improves Count Accuracy from 16.88\% to \textbf{28.44\%} and Effective Temporal F1 from 12.29\% to \textbf{16.86\%}. However, forcing distant multi-event clusters into one window retains background frames between events, limiting tIoU to 36.81\%. While splitting intervals without continuity merging (strategy c) can fragment adjacent actions, our proposed Adaptive SGDE with 70\% continuity merging (strategy d) groups proximate clusters while isolating distant intervals, improving tIoU to \textbf{44.76\%}, Count Accuracy to \textbf{33.12\%}, and Effective Temporal F1 to \textbf{19.14\%}.

(b) Background duration pruned: achieving 37.17\% background pruning across the benchmark.}

\paragraph{3. Timeline Scoring Function Evaluation.} As shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:scoring_ablation}, raw cosine similarity with a fixed cutoff is sensitive to baseline shifts across videos, resulting in 25.1\% missed action spans. Video-adaptive $Z$-score normalization with dual-threshold search and energy scoring $\bar{J}(a,b)$ improves ground-truth coverage from 70.9\% to \textbf{80.3\%}, improves proposal IoU from 34.2\% to \textbf{48.6\%}, and reduces missed action spans by 31.1\% relative.

\paragraph{4. Merge Threshold Selection ($\rho$).} Fig.~\ref{fig:soft_merge_ablation} evaluates the sensitivity of the continuity merge threshold $\rho$ from 0.30 to 1.00. Low thresholds ($\rho \le 0.40$) merge almost all proposals into a single window, maintaining continuous context but pruning fewer background frames (33.3\%). Conversely, high thresholds ($\rho \ge 0.90$) or gap-only splitting divide multiple videos into disjoint prompts, which degrades relational context and lowers Effective Temporal F1 to 16.81\%. Setting $\rho = 0.70$ provides an effective empirical trade-off: maintaining a single continuous window for 95.0\% of videos while capturing 79.71\% of ground-truth action spans and removing 37.17\% of background frames overall.
