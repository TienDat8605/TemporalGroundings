\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related_work}

\subsection{Video Temporal Grounding: Single-Event to One-to-Many}
Video Temporal Grounding (VTG) aims to localize temporal boundaries in an untrimmed video corresponding to a natural language query~\cite{gao2017tall,anne2017localizing}. Early approaches explored cross-modal proposal ranking~\cite{gao2017tall} and anchor regression~\cite{lei2021momentdetr}. The introduction of the QVHighlights benchmark~\cite{lei2021qvhighlights} expanded the field toward joint moment retrieval and highlight detection. However, standard datasets such as Charades-STA~\cite{gao2017tall} and ActivityNet-Captions~\cite{anne2017localizing} enforce a single-instance setting. To evaluate recurring actions, Xu \emph{et al.}~\cite{omtg2026} introduced One-to-Many Temporal Grounding (OMTG), revealing that standard video grounders and multimodal LLMs struggle on multi-instance queries. In this work, we present a training-free framework designed specifically for multi-event grounding.

\subsection{Multimodal Large Language Models for Video Understanding}
Recent Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs), such as Video-LLaVA~\cite{lin2023videollava}, VTimeLLM~\cite{li2023vtimellm}, Qwen2-VL~\cite{bai2024qwen2vl}, and TimeLens-8B~\cite{timelens2024}, demonstrate strong zero-shot temporal comprehension. TimeLens-8B incorporates dedicated coordinate tokens to predict continuous timestamps in text. Nonetheless, when processing long untrimmed videos, standard MLLMs sample visual tokens uniformly across the entire video. In long videos with extended background segments, uniform sampling dilutes visual attention and causes false positive predictions in empty intervals. Our work shows that restructuring prompt syntax recovers latent multi-span ability, while candidate window extraction removes irrelevant temporal regions.

\subsection{Training-Free and Coarse-to-Fine Video Grounding}
Processing long videos efficiently has motivated hierarchical coarse-to-fine strategies~\cite{lei2021momentdetr,lin2023unitime}. In contrast to approaches requiring gradient fine-tuning or model changes, training-free methods route computation dynamically at inference time. ScoutTG uses a lightweight 1.04B embedding scout operating at 1.0~FPS to scan the timeline, followed by duration-adaptive windowing and continuity merging. This decouples coarse temporal search from fine boundary decoding, reducing 8B Vision Transformer frame evaluations by 60.3\% while improving localization and count accuracy.
